LTspice automation / Calculation audit / 01

The waveform was right.
The averaging was wrong.

LTspice saves samples at uneven time intervals. The old code gave every saved point an equal vote. That let the recording schedule change the reported mean and RMS of the same signal.

One ramp. Different sample spacing.

The shaded area is the voltage integrated over time. Divide it by the 1-second duration to get the mean.

What the code reports

MetricOld codeCorrected
Mean
RMS

Before / count the points

Every sample gets the same weight.

mean = Σ Vᵢ / N
RMS = √(Σ Vᵢ² / N)

This is a sample statistic. It does not account for how long the signal spends between saved points.

After / account for time

Every segment contributes its area.

mean = Σ [Δt · (a + b) / 2] / T
RMS = √(Σ [Δt · (a² + ab + b²) / 3] / T)

Here a and b are adjacent voltages, Δt is their time gap, and T is the total duration. These formulas integrate the linear segments exactly, including the square used for RMS.

Verified ramp: 0 → 1 V in 1 second. Analytic mean = 0.5 V; RMS = 1/√3 ≈ 0.577350 V. Actual data: macOS LTspice 17.2.4, September 7, 2026. LTspice’s own .meas reported AVG 0.500 V and RMS 0.577343 V; the small RMS difference reflects numerical approximation. The other two presets are illustrative samples of the same ideal ramp.
